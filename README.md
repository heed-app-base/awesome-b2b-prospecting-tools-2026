## A buyer's five-step test

1. **Write the audience before shopping.** Specify geography, industry, company size, role, exclusions, and the number of contacts you can realistically work. “B2B leads” is too broad to evaluate.
2. **Define a usable row.** Decide which fields must be present for your campaign. If your team needs both an email path and a phone path, write that requirement explicitly. Decide whether a company main line is acceptable or whether you need a direct line or mobile number.
3. **Ask when and how data is checked.** Request the source URL, check date, field definitions, and the process for defects. “Verified” can refer to different checks and does not mean a person will answer or engage.
4. **Model the whole cost.** Include subscription or order price, credits, seats, staff time, and the number of rows meeting your own acceptance rule. For example, a hypothetical $100 order yielding 80 usable rows costs $1.25 per usable row ($100 ÷ 80). Those figures illustrate the calculation; they are not results for any provider here.
5. **Review a small, representative batch.** Inspect duplicates, role and company fit, missing fields, phone type, and format before scaling. Keep outreach permissions and applicable rules in your own review process.

## Terms worth defining in any quote

- **Accepted row:** A record that meets the seller's written delivery fields and eligibility rules. Get those rules in writing before comparing prices.
- **Verified work email:** A business email checked under the seller's stated method. It does not guarantee delivery to an inbox or a response.
- **Checked business phone:** A business contact number that may be a direct line or company main line unless the quote says otherwise.
- **Reveal or export credit:** A metered action in some prospecting platforms. Ask what triggers a charge and whether repeat access costs another credit.
- **Intent signal:** An indicator used to prioritize accounts. It is not proof that an identified person is ready to purchase.
- **Enrichment:** Adding or updating fields on existing contact or company records, often through a CRM or API workflow.
